--- layout: gallery_base title: "Jiggle Physics" eleventyNavigation: key: Jiggle Physics title: Jiggle Physics parent: Examples tags: example --- In order to cross-examine jiggle physics in The Fatal Fury it is important to have a familiarity with the franchise. Fatal Fury is a franchise of fighting games that began in 1991 with Fatal Fury: King of Fighters (Bankhurst, 2023). Originally an arcade game, Fatal Fury: King of Fighters and following sequels were quickly available on consoles including SNES, PC Engine CD, and Gameboy (Thorpe, 2022). Mai Shiranui debuted as a character in 1992 in Fatal Fury 2. She has since become an integral character in the franchise who has been both celebrated and problematized for her exemplification of the games’ use of jiggle physics. This controversial presence is exemplified by Super Smash Bro’s Ultimate director Masahiro Sakura’s decision to exclude Mai Shiranui from the game on an ethical basis, specifically stating that “the game is for “good boys and girls of many different ages”” (Lee, 2019). As an Asian woman character, it is important to consider how racism and sexism may intersect in Mai Shiranui. Celine Parreñas Shimizu discusses this in her book, The Hypersexuality of Race: Performing Asian/American Women on Screen and Scene. In this text, Shimizu grapples with the “injuries, harms and injustices” within the sexualization of Asian women in film, while also holding space for “eroticism” as a way to “enable a more autonomous subject formation for Asian women” (2007, p. 4).
