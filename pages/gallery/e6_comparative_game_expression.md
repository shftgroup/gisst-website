This paper is a discussion and exploration of some games on the Game and Interactive Software  Scholarship Toolkit (GISST) which is a Web emulation platform. I will be elaborating on my experience  of playing these historical video games and my observations. Furthermore, I will expand on the  motivation behind my game choices, the challenges faced working on the GISST platform, and proposed improvements for the platform. 

To begin, what is GISST? “The Game and Interactive Software Scholarship Toolkit (GISST) is a US National  Endowment for the Humanities (NEH) funded project to develop a framework for the citation and  sharing of emulated states and input streams. GISST allows a user to load files and games into an  emulator (either locally or in-browser), record their interactions with it, and then share linkable citations  to “replays” of user input as well as the emulator’s underlying memory states.” (Source). 

Games Explored 

I am an avid Soccer lover, which prompted me to explore some Soccer games on the GISST platform,  precisely focusing on FIFA soccer games. I also played some other popular games I remember playing  when I was younger, e.g., Super Mario, Space Invader, Chess, and Mortal Kombat. This exercise was  nostalgic. 

Comparing Old Games to newer Versions 

Reflecting on some differences between historical games in comparison to contemporary games, it is  notable to emphasize the games industry and how video games have grown and evolved.

Game Graphics 

As a soccer lover, I must say the game graphics for the historical FIFA games explored were low resolution 2d and pixelated, compared to more recent games which are 3D and high resolution. This  observation is similar to my observation of most of the games on the GISST platform, which seems  typical since they are old games. The characters in the Soccer game were not identifiable. The graphics  quality is low compared to the recent FIFA game. Furthermore, the video range was also very narrow,  during gameplay the full length of the pitch wasn’t visible which might limit the gamer's vision of the  soccer pitch or environment. In more contemporary Soccer games, there are different camera angle  options for the convenience of the player. 

User Experience 

My experience of playing the old Nintendo FIFA amongst other video games I explored on GISST was  purely nostalgic. Although, it took some time to adjust to the old FIFA in terms of the game speed,  controls, and responsiveness. I was still impressed with the responsiveness of the FIFA games on GISST. The game speed was decent and the input instructions from the controls were responded to at a  reasonable time. Amongst the different games I explored on GISST, the Battle Chess game I explored,  had a slow responsiveness, but I found amusing the battle mechanics designed in the game that make the chess pieces fight and kill the opponent's piece that was killed on the chessboard. I found this as an  interesting implementation of what the Chess game stands for, which is war and strategy. 

Conclusion 

A major observation I made during my exploration and comparison of Photoshop design software during  my Local and Cloud emulation exercises, is the stability and consistency in design and tool functions on  Photoshop over the years, the foundational experience of using the old Photoshop was almost similar 

to the new Photoshop. With respect to FIFA gameplay and experience, I believe there was a significant  shift in the experience and immersive state of the FIFA game. The contemporary FIFA is more cinematic,  although there is still some consistency in the game mechanics over time. 

Secondly, the game audio experience in the FIFA game has evolved from just old tunes to actual popular  recording artist music in the contemporary game, even the in-game commentary is more realistic in the  contemporary FIFA games. 

Lastly, I couldn’t help but notice some “Serialization” concepts explained in “Shane Denson’s” “Visualizing Digital Seriality or: All Your Mods Are Belong to Us!” while exploring the different FIFA games  and other games on GISST. The Inter-Ludic Seriality – the serialization dynamics that occur with game series. On GISST it was interesting to observe a single game had a series even unique to regions. E.g  Mortal Kombat II (USA), Mortal Kombat II (Europe), Mortal Kombat II (Japan), etc. Also, the Intra-Ludic  seriality- which processes the variation and repetition that takes place in-game. E.g. the different Super  Mario game levels and difficulty. 

A short reflection on GISST 

I found the GISST platform to be very exciting, especially because I got the opportunity to explore or try  out different games I had not heard of before and also play some older game versions/ series I played  when I was younger, e.g., Super Mario, Space Invader, Mortal Kombat, FIFA, amongst others. 

On an explorative phase of GISST, I somewhat got stuck at playing the games after boot up. I initially  assumed “Start Performance” meant start the game, but when the game didn’t launch, I had to consult  Dr. Eric Kaltman one of the designers of the GISST platform. Upon consultation, he helped resolve this  issue and directed me to simply click the pulsating icon on the interface. For a novice or first-time user,

this workflow might be confusing. I would suggest a text supporting the pulsating icon explicitly  instructing the user to start the game or the game launches automatically after booting up. 

Image of Game launch interface without a prompt 

Furthermore, I observed I couldn’t rewind or fast forward replays on the GISST platform, I believe this  would be a very nice feature, also, a timeline of how long the replay would take while watching could  improve the replay experience too. Another observation I made was while renaming my saved states  and performances, if I made a typing error, I couldn’t move my cursor to edit the misspelled word, I needed to delete letters and words till I got to the misspelled word before I could edit the sentence  properly. This was a little frustrating. Lastly, being able to access all my saved states and recorded  performances in a single folder or portfolio page will greatly improve the user experience on this  platform. 

Comparing this web emulation project to the Local and Cloud Emulation projects I initially executed on  Oracle VM VirtualBox and EaaSi respectively, I would say this was the easiest and most self-explanatory  emulation task. However, I think based on my experience of the three (3) emulation projects and using 

these different emulation platforms, I didn’t have the flexibility and affordances to install my chosen  software and historical games compared to the freedom I got on VirtualBox and EaaSI. 

In conclusion, apart from our source code, software, and hardware archiving while storing and  recovering historical software and games, it is also important not to neglect the look and feel of these  games, and the immersive experience of these games. We therefore need records of performance to  get a wholesome experience of each game. This is one of the benefits of GISST. The replays and  recordings are important artifacts that can be saved and cited for game documentation.
